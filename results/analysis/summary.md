## (c) Break-even α: where agreement stops paying

From the Best-of-N samples (64 per prompt, both ratings on every sample), with the mixed reward (1 − α)·immediate + α·informed. *Raw gap*: α* = g_a / (g_a + g_i), g_a the agreeable user's rating of agreeing minus gold samples, g_i the informed user's rating of gold minus agreeing samples, wrong-opinion prompts. *Push* (the 64-sample estimate): GRPO's group-normalized advantage, mean over agreeing samples minus mean over gold samples (*vs gold*) or over every other sample (*vs rest*, the sign of the net advantage on agreement), zero crossing on a 0.01 grid, groups of 64 (a prompt) or 8 (as in training); *opinionated*: following the user minus the rest, wrong- and correct-opinion prompts pooled. *BoN*: Best-of-64 sycophancy minus a random sample's, zero crossing on a 0.05 grid. *Form*: bare = under 20 characters before the last `Answer:` (or in total), long = 200+ characters in total, medium = the rest; the push of a form normalizes over the whole group, as training does. 95% bootstrap over questions; n/a = fewer than 20 samples on a side.

The samples are Best-of-N's (top-p 0.95 with the model's default top-k, bfloat16), not GRPO's (top-p 1, top-k 0, float32): the push is the 64-sample estimate on another sampler, not training's step-0 push (see Caveats).

## Caveats

- **Post hoc.** The thresholds (onset at 64% / 59%, short episodes under 35 tokens, reply forms at 20 / 200 characters, the proxy-gain and √KL grids) were set after these runs had been seen. The numbers describe the runs; they are not tests of pre-registered hypotheses.
- **Runs are the unit, and there are few.** Two to four runs per condition. A percentile bootstrap over two or three runs cannot leave the range of the observed values, so its intervals show the spread of the runs and understate the uncertainty about a new run.
- **First-crossing reads drop runs.** A run that never reaches a threshold is left out of that threshold's mean, so means at high proxy gain or √KL describe the runs that got there (survivorship), and the number of runs reached is part of the result.
- **Evaluation noise.** Every point is one greedy evaluation of 300 validation questions per opinion type, every 25 steps: about ±3 points of binomial noise on sycophancy, and points of one run are correlated.
- **KL is the trainer's per-token estimate**, averaged over the 25 steps before an evaluation. It is noisy and not a sequence-level KL; when completions shrink to a letter, per-token and sequence KL drift apart.
- **Selection and training differ in data and decoding.** Best-of-N uses temperature-1 samples of the base model on 200 test questions; GRPO is evaluated greedily on 300 validation questions. They are compared through changes from their own start.
- **The break-even describes the base model.** It is computed on the base model's samples, i.e. the reward landscape at step 0; the policy's distribution, and with it the local break-even, moves during training. The push ignores the KL penalty, clipping and the token-level loss normalization, and treats a prompt's samples as one or eight groups.
- **The push is a 64-sample estimate on another sampler.** The Best-of-N samples were drawn with top-p 0.95 and the model's default top-k (Qwen3: 20) in bfloat16; GRPO's rollouts used top-p 1 and top-k 0 in float32. The Best-of-N sampler cuts the tail of unlikely replies, so the push here is not training's step-0 push; a TRL-matched step-0 value will come from the base policy's samples of the `sample_rate` stage (M3), which uses the rollout settings.
- **One judge** (one model family) gives every rating; Best-of-N picks here are expected picks with ties averaged, which differ slightly from the committed single-draw tie-breaks (checked in `frontier.json`, `bon_check`).
